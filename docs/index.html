Background

Nivedita Bhadra trained as a physicist and carried that quantitative foundation into biomedical research and statistical genetics, building models that have to hold up against real, messy, large-scale data.

She is currently at the Institute of Biological Psychiatry in Denmark, where she builds statistical genetics models for risk prediction on large-scale cohort data. The work covers liability-scale modeling, polygenic risk scores, and related quantitative genetics methods. Before that, she spent several years at the Translational Genomics Research Institute (TGen), applying high-dimensional statistical modeling to biomedical and clinical data.

Her path into this work started in physics: an M.Sc. from IIT Delhi, followed by a PhD in Computational Physics from IISER Kolkata, where she built numerical models of complex physical systems. Simulation, statistical inference, and large-scale computation connect everything since, whether the system is physical, genomic, or textual.

Her core focus is population genomics and statistical genetics. She computes polygenic risk scores at scale using SBayesR via GCTB, including a large-scale analysis of iPSYCH2015 (N=105,477) that generated roughly 10,000 PGS from FinnGen and UK Biobank summary statistics. She also works on causal inference in pharmacogenomics, using genetic variants as instruments in Mendelian randomization, and has built Martingale residual transformations to convert Cox models into linear GWAS.

Outside genomics, she applies predictive modeling (LASSO, random forest, XGBoost, ensemble and Super Learner methods) to clinical trial and biomedical data. She has also worked with phylogenetic comparative methods (PGLS, MDMR), leading transcriptome-lifespan association analyses across species while controlling for phylogenetic non-independence.

Day to day, she works in Python, R, and SQL. She uses scikit-learn and XGBoost for predictive modeling, and Hugging Face’s pipeline API (BERT, DistilBERT, Sentence-BERT, BERTopic) with PyTorch for GPU-accelerated inference on HPC systems. She builds reproducible pipelines with Snakemake, Docker, and Singularity on Linux/HPC, and has built and deployed end-to-end ML pipelines, from model training through FastAPI, documented in technical write-ups on Medium.
